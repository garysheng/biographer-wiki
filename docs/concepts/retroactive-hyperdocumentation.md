# Retroactive Hyperdocumentation

*Agentic biography is hyperdocumentation run backward, on someone else's behalf: reconstructing, from what survives (memory told aloud, photos, videos, documents, other people's accounts), the annotated record the person never kept as they lived.*

---

## The same discipline, pointed at the past

[Hyperdocumentation](https://hyperdocumentation.com/concepts/hyperdocumentation) is the practice of capturing, annotating, owning and projecting the record of a life as it happens, so an AI can remember it and make anything out of it. Almost nobody alive today did that for the decades already behind them. Their record exists, scattered: a shoebox of prints, a phone full of videos, a folder of deeds and letters, a sister who remembers the move differently, and the person's own memory, which is the richest source and the only one that has to be asked.

Agentic biography runs the same four operations on that scattered record:

- **Capture.** Record the person telling it, and gather what survived: photos, videos, voice memos, scanned documents, other people's accounts.
- **Annotate.** Give every piece its context, so an agent reading it cold knows what it is.
- **Own.** Keep it as plain files in a tree the family controls, the [biographical ontology](/ontology/the-biographical-ontology), rather than inside any one app.
- **Project.** Render the forms the family wants from that tree, as [projections](/concepts/forms-are-projections).

Two things change. The time runs backward: you are reconstructing years that are over. And the subject is someone else: the workspace is built on their behalf, usually by a child, a friend or a hired biographer working with an agent. Everything else about the discipline holds.

## The atoms

A biography is assembled from small units, and the quality of every form depends on how well those units are made. The atoms are the shapes of the ontology:

- **Transcripts**: what the person said, verbatim, with the question that drew it out.
- **Characters**: the recurring people.
- **Moments**: bounded things that happened, at whatever precision memory supports. See [Moments and the Timeline](/ontology/moments-and-the-timeline).
- **Letters**: what the biographer asked.
- **Media**: the photos, videos, voice memos and scanned documents that survived, each with an annotation beside it.
- **External events**: the history the person lived through (a war, a flood, a factory closing, a moon landing). These are moments tagged `external`, which lets the life be read against the world it happened in.

The profile and the timeline sit over the atoms: one orients the agent, the other indexes the moments.

## Every atom carries its own context

A raw atom is almost useless to an agent. A photo named `IMG_0412.jpg` says nothing about who is in it, when it was taken, or which summer it belongs to. A transcript line that says "that was the year we lost the house" does not say which house or which year.

So every atom is **hypercontextualized**: it answers, on its own, the [five questions](https://hypercontext.wiki/start-here#the-five-question-test).

1. **What is it about?** The subject, in words an agent can search.
2. **When was it true?** A date at the precision the evidence supports.
3. **Who said or made it?** The teller, the photographer, the sister whose account this is.
4. **Who may see it?** Some memories are for the grandchildren; some are for no one yet.
5. **What does it connect to?** The characters in it, the moments it belongs to, the transcript that mentions it.

Once the atoms carry their context, assembly is the cheap part. An agent can compose a printed book, a narrated site, a ninetieth-birthday toast or a video tribute from the same tree, because it can select by person, by decade, by audience, and trace every claim back to its source. The years go into annotation. The forms come almost free.

## How it differs from hyperdocumenting forward

- **Precision is whatever memory supports.** A forward record is stamped to the minute by the device that captured it. A retroactive one has to accept `1954`, or "the summer before school", and declare that precision honestly. Inventing a day the person never gave corrupts the timeline.
- **The question is part of the record.** Forward capture mostly records what happened. Retroactive capture records a person being asked, so every answer is shaped by its question, and [the question is filed with it](/principles/the-question-is-part-of-the-record).
- **The subject blesses.** The agent can draft an annotation, guess a date from a photo, or merge two tellings into one moment. The person whose life it is decides whether that reading is true. While they are alive to ask, their word settles it; the agent's inference is a candidate.
- **Sources disagree, and both stay.** Two siblings remember the same afternoon differently. Each account is filed as its own atom and linked to the same moment. Resolving them is a projection's editorial choice, never a deletion in the corpus.

## Keep the atoms few

The pull, once you see a life as atoms, is to invent more kinds of them. Resist it. The atoms above cover almost everything a family will ever render, and each new shape costs the agent something on every cycle. Media earned its place because a retroactive biography is built mostly from what survived; external events did not need a new shape at all. The rule is in [Don't Overengineer the Ontology](/principles/dont-overengineer-the-ontology).

> **A biography is the hyperdocumentation a person never kept, rebuilt on their behalf from what survived. Annotate every atom well and any form can be assembled from it.**
